# DriverPass Project Reflection

**Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?**

For this project, our client was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students better prepare for their driving tests. They needed an online system where customers could purchase training packages, schedule driving lessons, access study materials, and take practice exams. The system also needed to help employees manage appointments, customer information, and other daily operations. Our goal was to design something that would make the process easier for both customers and employees.

**What did you do particularly well?**

I think one thing I did particularly well was breaking down the client's requirements and turning them into a system design that was easier to understand. I was able to identify the main features DriverPass needed and show how they would work through different UML diagrams. I also think I did a good job considering how customers and employees would interact with the system instead of only focusing on the technical side.

**If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?**

If I could revise one part of my work, I would probably focus on my UML sequence diagram. Although it shows how the scheduling process works, I think some parts could be organized better to make it easier to follow. I would simplify a few of the interactions and make sure the different steps are clearly separated. This would help someone unfamiliar with the project understand how the system handles scheduling without having to spend too much time figuring out the diagram.

**How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?**

I started by reviewing the DriverPass interview and identifying what the client wanted the system to accomplish. From there, I separated the requirements into different features, such as scheduling lessons, managing training packages, processing payments, and providing access to practice exams. I then used those requirements to create diagrams that showed how the system would operate. I think considering the user's needs is one of the most important parts of designing software because even if a system works perfectly from a technical standpoint, it won't be very useful if it doesn't solve the problems it was created for.

**How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?**

When designing software, I like to start by understanding the problem before trying to figure out how to build the solution. I think it's important to gather requirements, identify who will be using the system, and break larger processes into smaller steps. In the future, I would continue using tools like UML diagrams to visualize how different parts of a system interact. I would also make sure to communicate with the client throughout the design process and get feedback early so any misunderstandings can be addressed before development begins.
